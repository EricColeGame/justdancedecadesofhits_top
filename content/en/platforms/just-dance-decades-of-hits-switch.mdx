### Code-in-Box vs. a Physical Cartridge

This is the single most misunderstood part of the release, so here's a side-by-side.

| Aspect | Code-in-Box | Physical game card |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What you receive | A printed redemption code | A cartridge with game data |
| Resale value | Generally none once redeemed | Can be resold or traded |
| Storage | Download counts against internal storage or microSD | Still needs space for patches and updates |
| Lending | Tied to the account that redeemed it | Cart can be passed around |
| Internet | Required to redeem, and per the publisher, to play | Often playable offline |
| Best for | Digital-first households and gifting | Collectors and shared consoles |

If your household shares one Switch between several people, a cartridge is usually more flexible. If everyone has their own profile and a Nintendo account, the code route is perfectly fine — you just need to decide which account redeems it, because that's the account that owns the game.

## Just Dance Decades of Hits Switch Download: Redeeming Your Code

Because there's no cartridge, setup is a short but specific process. Skipping a step here is the most common reason people think their code is broken.

| Step | Action | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Confirm you have a code, not a card | Code-in-Box products contain no game card |
| 2 | Connect the Switch to the internet | Redemption happens online through the eShop |
| 3 | Sign in to your Nintendo account | The game is permanently tied to this account |
| 4 | Enter the redemption code in the eShop | Codes are typically 16 characters |
| 5 | Download and install the game | Check free space on internal storage or microSD |
| 6 | Link or create a Ubisoft account on first launch | Required for online features and content access |

Two requirements deserve emphasis. First, an internet connection is needed to redeem the code, and the publisher notes a persistent connection is required for the online side of the experience. Second, both a Ubisoft account and a Nintendo account are involved — don't skip the Ubisoft sign-in, or you may not see the content tied to your profile.

If your code won't redeem, the usual culprits are a mistyped character (0 versus O, 1 versus I), a region mismatch between the code and your eShop account, or a code that was already used. In player experience, most redemption failures trace back to one of those three.

## FAQ

**Is Just Dance: Decades of Hits on Switch a physical game?**

No — the Limited Edition is a Code-in-Box product. You receive a download code rather than a game card, so you'll need internet access, a Nintendo account, and a Ubisoft account to redeem and play it.

**Do I need an internet connection to play the Just Dance Decades of Hits Switch version?**

Yes, at least for redemption, and the publisher notes that a persistent internet connection is required for the online experience. Plan for a stable home network rather than relying on offline play.

**What does the Limited Edition include beyond the game?**

The Limited Edition adds the 2000s Digital Cosmetic Pack, which customizes your Dancer Card and scoring effects with early-2000s-inspired items. These are cosmetic and don't affect scoring or difficulty.

**How many songs are in the game?**

The publisher hasn't confirmed a final track count in the retail description. What is confirmed is that the game spans over six decades, with each era getting its own visual identity and curated hitlist. Wait for Ubisoft's official announcement before treating any leaked list as final.

**Can I share the game with family members?**

Because the code is tied to the Nintendo account that redeems it, sharing works differently than with a cartridge. Everyone can play on the same console using their own profile, but the game itself belongs to the redeeming account.
