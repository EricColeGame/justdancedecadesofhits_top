## What Is Just Dance: Decades of Hits?

Just Dance: Decades of Hits is a themed Just Dance experience built around eras instead of a single year. Ubisoft's own landing page for the game captures the concept in a four-word tagline: "Every Era. One Floor." The tracklist is grouped by the decade each song defined — 70s, 80s, 90s, 2000s, and 2010s — and the music does the time travel.

That structure matters more than it first appears. Just Dance tracklists have always mixed new hits with older favorites, but Decades of Hits makes the eras the organizing principle. That means the setlist is curated for contrast: a disco-era singalong lands next to an early hip-hop anthem, which lands next to a boy-band routine. You can read the [official Just Dance: Decades of Hits page from Ubisoft](https://www.ubisoft.com/en-us/game/just-dance/decades-of-hits) for the publisher's own era breakdown.

## Tips for Playing Through All Five Decades

- **Warm up in the 70s.** The older tracks tend to have wider, slower movements that loosen up shoulders and hips before the tempo climbs.
- **Save the 2010s for last.** Turn Down for What is a closer, not an opener — its intensity is hard to follow with anything gentler.
- **Alternate eras instead of playing them in order.** Jumping from the 70s to the 2000s and back keeps energy from flatlining.
- **Use the 90s as your reset.** Wannabe works as a palate cleanser because the chorus is easy and the room will sing over the dancing anyway.
- **Give new players the 1970s.** Lower-speed choreography with big silhouettes is much easier to mirror than tight hip-hop footwork.
- **Watch for alternate coaches.** Some routines in the Just Dance catalog ship with alternate performers, so check the song's coach select screen before assuming there's only one routine.
- **Rotate the phone or controller.** In group play, passing the tracking device between songs prevents one person from dominating the score.
- **Build a two-era mini-set first.** If your group is new to the game, run a 70s block and an 80s block before committing to all five.

## How the Decades Format Changes Party Play

Most Just Dance sessions are built around whatever is newest. Decades of Hits inverts that: the appeal is the spread, not the freshness. That has a real effect on how a room behaves.

A single-year tracklist tends to favor whoever knows current pop best. A five-decade tracklist spreads the advantage around. The person who grew up on ABBA gets a turn. So does the one who wore out a Run-DMC cassette. So does the teenager who only knows Turn Down for What. In practice, that makes the Just Dance Decades of Hits song list one of the more socially balanced setlists the franchise has assembled — nobody is dominant for more than two songs in a row.

It also changes how you should score the night. Chasing a high score across eras is harder than chasing one within a single genre, because the movement vocabulary shifts every few songs. Players who want a clean leaderboard run should pick one decade and stay in it. Players who want the actual Decades of Hits experience should move around.

## FAQ

**What songs are on the Just Dance Decades of Hits song list?**
The confirmed tracks span five decades and include Mamma Mia by ABBA, Come and Get Your Love by Redbone, and ABC by The Jackson 5 from the 1970s; Jump by Van Halen, Into the Groove by Madonna, and It's Tricky by Run-DMC from the 1980s; Just a Girl by No Doubt and Wannabe by the Spice Girls from the 1990s; Bye Bye Bye by *NSYNC from the 2000s; and Turn Down for What by DJ Snake and Lil Jon from the 2010s.

**Is the tracklist final, or will more songs be added?**
Fan wikis and shared playlists have floated extra names, including Sum 41's "In Too Deep" and Chappell Roan's "HOT TO GO!" Those come from community reports and prediction lists rather than confirmed official announcements, so treat them as possibilities rather than certainties until the publisher confirms them.

**Which era is easiest for beginners?**
The 1970s is the friendliest starting point. Songs like Mamma Mia and Come and Get Your Love use broad, slower movements that are easier to mirror than the tight footwork found in the 80s and 2010s entries. Once a new player has two or three 70s songs under their belt, moving into the 90s feels natural.

**Where can I find the official Just Dance: Decades of Hits tracklist?**
The safest sources are Ubisoft's own game page and any tracklist the publisher labels as official. Third-party playlists can be useful for discovery, but they frequently mix verified songs with speculation, so cross-check before treating a song as confirmed.
