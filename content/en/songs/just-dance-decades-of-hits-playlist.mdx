## What Makes the Just Dance Decades of Hits Playlist Different?

Most Just Dance entries organize their songs by release year, mixing eras at random. Decades of Hits flips that: every track is tagged with the decade it came from, so the song list reads like a timeline you can dance through. Ubisoft's own tagline for the game — "Every Era. One Floor." — sums up the pitch perfectly. You can read more on the [official Just Dance: Decades of Hits page on Ubisoft.com](https://www.ubisoft.com/en-us/game/just-dance/decades-of-hits).

That makes this particular playlist unusually friendly for parties, for mixed-age groups, and for players who haven't picked up a motion controller in years.

## How to Build Your Own Decades of Hits Playlist

If you're running the night, the order of songs matters as much as the songs themselves. Here's a structure that works for a 45–60 minute session.

| Step | What to Do | Why It Works |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Open with two 1970s tracks | Low difficulty, high recognition, builds confidence |
| 2 | Move to one 1980s or 1990s song | Raises tempo without exhausting anyone |
| 3 | Drop a slower 2000s groove | Recovery beat before the finale |
| 4 | Stack two 2010s bangers back to back | Peak energy while everyone's still fresh |
| 5 | Close with a group routine | Ends on a shared moment, not a solo loss |

A few extra rules of thumb from player experience:

- **Cap each decade at three songs.** The full list is only 35 tracks — blowing through one era leaves the rest feeling like filler.
- **Alternate solo and group routines.** Solo songs create leaderboard drama; group songs keep everyone involved.
- **Save the hardest track for slot four or five.** Stamina peaks early, so putting "Turn Down for What" first wastes it.
- **Repeat one song at the end if the room demands it.** Nostalgia runs on encores.

## Tips for Hosting a Decades Dance Night

The playlist does most of the work, but a few hosting choices push a good night into a great one.

- **Assign decades to teams.** Give each group a decade and let them pick their own anthem from that block.
- **Keep the room dark-ish and the volume high.** The era-specific staging and coach outfits read better on a bigger screen.
- **Use a scoreboard, even a crude one.** A whiteboard tally turns casual play into a tournament.
- **Plan a snack break around the 2000s block.** It's the natural midpoint of a chronological run.
- **Record one song per decade.** Ten years from now, that footage is the whole point.

If you're building a companion streaming playlist for the car ride over, note that official soundtrack listings and community playlists don't always match the in-game lineup exactly. Ubisoft's own era breakdown is the safest reference for what you'll actually dance to.

## FAQ

**How many songs are in the Just Dance Decades of Hits playlist?**
Community trackers count 35 songs in the base list, spanning the 1970s through the 2010s. That's smaller than a mainline numbered Just Dance game, but nearly every track is a widely recognized hit, which makes the Just Dance Decades of Hits playlist easier to play start to finish in one sitting.

**Which decade has the easiest songs?**
The 1970s block, based on community-reported difficulty. "ABC" by The Jackson 5 is widely described as one of the simplest routines in the game, and it's a trio number, so three players can learn it together.

**Can I build my own Just Dance Decades of Hits playlist outside the game?**
Yes. Several community-curated Spotify playlists mirror much of the soundtrack, and they're handy for warm-ups, car rides, or parties where the console isn't set up yet. Just expect minor differences — some listings include radio edits and extras that aren't part of Ubisoft's official era highlights.

**What's the best song to finish a party with?**
A group routine beats a solo every time. "Wannabe," "Mamma Mia," and "Bye Bye Bye" all end a night on a shared, chaotic, sing-along note — which is the entire point of a decades-themed dance party.
