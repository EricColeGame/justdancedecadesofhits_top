## What Is Just Dance: Decades of Hits?

Just Dance: Decades of Hits is the latest mainline entry in the Just Dance franchise, a series that has been putting players in front of the camera since its first release back in November 2009. Instead of the usual numbered annual edition, this one is built around a concept: every era gets its own floor. Ubisoft's own tagline for the game — "Every Era. One Floor" — sums up the pitch nicely.

The core loop hasn't changed. You follow on-screen choreography, your movements are tracked, and you get scored on accuracy and timing. What's new is the framing. Rather than a single contemporary playlist, the game organizes its content as a journey through six decades of pop, disco, rock, and dance music, with visuals and staging that shift to match each period.

## Six Decades of Hits: What Each Era Brings

The marketing promise of six decades is easy to say and harder to deliver. What makes this entry interesting is that each era isn't just a playlist label — it comes with its own presentation, so the decade you pick changes how the game looks and feels, not just what you hear.

| Decade | Signature Vibe | What to Expect on the Floor |
|---|---|---|
| 1970s | Disco and early funk | Big, theatrical arm lines and mirrored-floor staging |
| 1980s | Synth-pop and new wave | Neon color palettes and punchy, pose-heavy choreography |
| 1990s | Pop, dance, and hip-hop crossover | Group formations and call-and-response style moves |
| 2000s | Club anthems and pop hooks | Fast footwork with lots of repeated crowd-pleaser beats |
| 2010s | EDM and streaming-era pop | Big drops, jump sections, and high-energy transitions |
| 2020s | Modern pop and viral dance culture | Moves that feel lifted straight from short-form video trends |

A few things stand out when you look at that spread:

- **The '70s and '80s routines tend to be the most theatrical.** Disco choreography rewards big, committed arm movements, which is great for players who like to perform rather than just score.
- **The '90s and 2000s are the sweet spot for mixed groups.** The moves are rhythmic and repetitive enough that someone who has never played can pick them up within a song or two.
- **The 2010s and 2020s skew toward stamina.** Expect jump-heavy sections and faster transitions, which is where younger or more practiced players tend to pull ahead.
- **Era hopping is the real feature.** Playing three songs from three different decades back-to-back is a genuinely different experience from playing three from the same one.

Community chatter ahead of launch suggested the decade gimmick was the single biggest reason people were interested — nostalgia sells, and a game that lets a parent and a teenager both recognize the tracklist has obvious household appeal.

## How to Jump In on Day One

Now that the Just Dance Decades of Hits release is live, the setup is straightforward. Here's a practical order of operations if you want to be dancing within fifteen minutes of opening the box or finishing the download.

| Step | What to Do | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Confirm your platform and patch the game | Day-one updates usually fix tracking calibration issues |
| 2 | Set up your camera or controller tracking | Poor lighting or a bad angle ruins scoring more than bad dancing does |
| 3 | Calibrate before your first song | Two minutes here saves you a night of confusing scores |
| 4 | Start with a decade you already know | Familiar songs make the tracking feel intuitive faster |
| 5 | Create a short "greatest hits" playlist across eras | Best way to show new players what the game can do |
| 6 | Invite at least three people | Group play is where the format shines |

A few extra tips that make a real difference:

- **Clear the space behind you, not just in front.** Tracking systems care about silhouette, and a cluttered background causes dropouts.
- **Play two songs, then rotate.** Dancing through a full era in one sitting is exhausting for newcomers.
- **Use the '70s and '80s tracks as a warm-up.** The slower, broader movements are kinder to your legs than a 2010s EDM drop.
- **Don't chase perfect scores on night one.** Accuracy improves dramatically once you stop thinking about the moves and start anticipating them.
