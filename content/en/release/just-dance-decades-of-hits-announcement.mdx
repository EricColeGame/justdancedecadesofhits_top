## Why the Just Dance Decades of Hits Announcement Still Matters

The Just Dance Decades of Hits announcement promised something the franchise had never really attempted before: one dance floor that stretches from the 1970s all the way to the 2020s. For a series that spent more than fifteen years naming every mainline game after a single calendar year, that is a genuine change of direction.

Instead of chasing whatever topped the charts last season, Decades of Hits treats pop history itself as the setlist. That is why the Just Dance Decades of Hits announcement generated so much discussion among longtime players — it signals that the series is now curating an archive rather than a yearly snapshot.

Ubisoft's tagline for the project, "Every Era. One Floor," captures the idea neatly on the [official Just Dance: Decades of Hits page](https://www.ubisoft.com/en-us/game/just-dance/decades-of-hits). The game launched on October 13, 2026, for Nintendo Switch, PlayStation 5, and Xbox Series X/S, and it arrives with a brand-new Adventure Mode sitting alongside the familiar song-and-score loop.

## The Just Dance Decades of Hits Announcement Timeline, Explained

That teaser set the tone for everything that followed: this is a game about nostalgia, so the marketing leaned into the look of the past rather than a bullet-point feature list.

## Six Decades, One Dance Floor: How the Tracklist Works

The organizing principle here is the decade, not the year. Ubisoft has described the lineup as iconic songs pulled from the 1970s, 1980s, 1990s, 2000s, 2010s, and 2020s, with each era bringing its own choreography vocabulary to the floor.

| Decade | Musical character | What it tends to mean on the dance floor |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1970s | Disco, funk, and early arena rock | Big arm lines, group formations, lots of repetition to learn quickly |
| 1980s | Synth-pop and new wave | Sharp, punchy moves with strong poses and dramatic silhouettes |
| 1990s | Pop, dance, and hip-hop crossovers | Faster footwork and more attitude-driven choreography |
| 2000s | Club pop and R&B | Tight, rhythmic combos built around hooks |
| 2010s | EDM and streaming-era pop | High-energy bursts and heavy chorus choreography |
| 2020s | Contemporary pop and viral hits | Short, shareable routines that reward replay |

The full song list has been published through Ubisoft's official channels and collected on the [Just Dance: Decades of Hits tracklist page](https://genius.com/albums/Ubisoft/Just-dance-decades-of-hits-official-tracklist), so you can plan a setlist before you turn the console on.

A useful tip: do not treat the decades as difficulty tiers. In practice, era and difficulty are separate axes. A 1970s disco track can have simple, looping choreography that a first-timer picks up in two attempts, while a 2020s pop routine can be genuinely demanding. Filter by difficulty, not by decade, when you are building a warm-up.

### Building a Balanced Session

1. **Start with two songs from the 1970s or 1980s.** The repetition builds confidence and gets everyone moving.
2. **Move into a 1990s or 2000s block.** This is where footwork picks up and the room gets loud.
3. **Finish with 2010s and 2020s tracks.** Save the fastest, most explosive routines for when you are already warm.
4. **Rotate the decade order next session.** The variety is the entire point of this entry.

## Adventure Mode and Practical Tips for New Players

Adventure Mode is the headline addition, and it is the clearest sign that Decades of Hits is not simply a greatest-hits playlist with a new cover.

| Feature | What it adds |
| --- | --- |
| Adventure Mode | A structured progression that gives solo players a reason to keep returning beyond high scores |
| Decade-based song pool | Six eras of music in one package, rather than a single year's chart |
| Classic scoring loop | The familiar routine-by-routine structure that longtime fans expect |
| Group-friendly design | Multiplayer sessions remain the core way most people will play |

For anyone jumping in for the first time, a few habits make the experience much better:

- **Calibrate your space first.** Clear a generous radius around each player; most injuries and broken lamps come from underestimating reach.
- **Use the phone controller for guests.** It lowers the barrier for friends who do not own the console.
- **Play in short sessions.** Twenty to thirty minutes, three times a week, beats one marathon.
- **Chase accuracy before speed.** The scoring system rewards clean movement timing more than frantic motion.
- **Try Adventure Mode solo, then bring it to a group.** It works as a personal progression track, but the routines translate well to a party.

The Just Dance Decades of Hits announcement ultimately promised breadth, and the delivered product matches that promise: six decades, one floor, and a structure that gives both solo players and party crowds something to do.
