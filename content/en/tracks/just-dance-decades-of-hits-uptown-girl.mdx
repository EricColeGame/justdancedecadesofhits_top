## Why "Uptown Girl" Anchors the Setlist

Billy Joel released "Uptown Girl" in 1983, and four decades later it still works as a universal crowd-pleaser. Wedding bands play it, film soundtracks quote it, and almost nobody needs the lyric sheet. That combination — instantly recognizable melody plus an upbeat tempo — is exactly what a Just Dance tracklist is looking for.

Within Just Dance: Decades of Hits, the song represents the 1980s next to Run-DMC's "It's Tricky," giving that decade two very different flavors: polished pop craftsmanship on one side, pioneering hip-hop on the other. It also gives older players a reason to pick up a controller and younger players a reason to discover a song their parents wore out.

## How to Nail the Just Dance Decades of Hits Uptown Girl Routine

The Just Dance Decades of Hits Uptown Girl choreography rewards timing more than athleticism. The song sits at a comfortable mid-tempo, so the danger is not running out of breath — it is drifting off the beat during the repeated verse sections where the movements look deceptively simple.

A short, structured practice session beats repeated full-speed attempts. Try this routine the next time you sit down to play:

| Step | What to do | Time |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Watch the coach twice without moving | 3 minutes |
| 2 | Learn only the chorus, at full speed | 5 minutes |
| 3 | Run the verses at half effort, focusing on hand placement | 5 minutes |
| 4 | Play the full song once, ignoring your score | 3 minutes |
| 5 | Play again and chase the "Super" rating | 3 minutes |

A few habits separate high scorers from everyone else. Keep your phone or controller in your dominant hand and mirror the coach rather than copying them literally — Just Dance coaches are mirrored on screen for a reason. Keep your feet moving even when you miss an arm cue, because the scoring window is more forgiving than it looks. And resist the urge to restart after one bad section; a single dropped phrase rarely costs you the star rating.

Player experience from earlier entries in the series also suggests that warming up on two easier songs before tackling a track you are struggling with improves accuracy noticeably, simply because your body is already synced to the game's timing.

## Everything New: Adventure Mode, Party Mode, and Workout Mode

Just Dance: Decades of Hits adds a story-driven mode for the first time in a while. Adventure Mode follows Lyra, the daughter of Dr. Gigavolt, through six decade-themed worlds filled with mini-activities, exclusive rewards, and dances built around era-defining songs. It is the closest the series has come to a single-player campaign, and it gives solo players something to do beyond chasing high scores.

The rest of the feature set is designed around groups.

| Mode or Feature | What it does | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Adventure Mode | Story journey through six decade-themed worlds with mini-activities and rewards | Solo sessions |
| Party Mode | Changes the rules every round | Chaotic group nights |
| Workout Mode | Tracks calories burned per dance | Fitness routines |
| Local multiplayer | Up to six dancers on one screen | Family and friends |
| Phone controller | Turns a smartphone into a controller | Avoiding extra hardware purchases |
| Dancer cards | Multiple profiles on one account | Shared consoles |

The phone-as-controller option deserves a special mention. Because it removes the need to buy extra accessories, a group of six can play with nothing but the devices already in their pockets. That single design choice makes Decades of Hits one of the more accessible party games on the market.
