## Coaching Tips: How to Prepare for the Chart

You don't need the full routine to start training. The song's structure tells you most of what you need to know.

| Focus area | Why it matters here | Practice drill |
|---|---|---|
| Bassline timing | The riff is the pulse; lock to it and everything else follows | Hum the bass figure while stepping side to side on each note |
| Half-time feel | The groove is slower than it sounds, so rushing is easy | Count slowly to four through the chorus instead of chasing the hi-hat |
| The laugh | A signature moment that likely reads as a held pose | Freeze cleanly on the beat rather than adding extra movement |
| Section changes | The texture shifts at least three times | Split the song into four blocks and rehearse only the transitions |
| Arms and upper body | Off-beat grooves lean heavily on arm styling | Keep elbows loose; sharp angles read better on camera |

A few broader habits help too:

- Play the track on repeat for a week before the routine drops so the arrangement feels predictable.
- Rehearse in short sessions — 10 to 15 minutes — rather than one long grind.
- If your version of the game includes a practice or slowed-down mode, use it on the chorus, which is where most players will lose their timing.
- Record yourself once. Watching a single take exposes rushing faster than any amount of repetition.
