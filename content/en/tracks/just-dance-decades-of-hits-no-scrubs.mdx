## How to Learn the Just Dance Decades of Hits No Scrubs Choreography

Trying to learn the whole routine in one pass is the fastest way to get frustrated. Break it into blocks instead, then stitch the blocks together once each one feels automatic.

### Block-by-Block Breakdown

1. **Intro block.** The opening bars are usually about establishing the groove — weight shifts, shoulder rolls, and a relaxed stance. Treat this as your calibration window.
2. **Verse block.** Arm patterns dominate here. Keep your elbows soft and your hands crisp; stiff arms read as sloppy even when your timing is right.
3. **Chorus block.** This is the highest-scoring section because it repeats. Nail it once and you will bank points on every return.
4. **Bridge and outro block.** Poses tend to hold longer here. Freeze cleanly instead of drifting out of position early.

### A Practice Plan That Actually Works

| Session | Focus | Suggested Time | Goal |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Watch the routine at full speed | 10 minutes | Absorb the overall shape and flow |
| 2 | Intro and verse blocks only | 15 minutes | Execute without looking at the screen constantly |
| 3 | Chorus block on repeat | 15 minutes | Build automatic recall of the highest-value section |
| 4 | Full run, no pausing | 10 minutes | Identify where your timing breaks down |
| 5 | Full run with styling | 10 minutes | Add attitude, facial expression, and clean finishes |

Most players notice a real difference after three or four focused sessions. The routine is not long, and because the song loops familiar patterns, retention is high.

### Match the Attitude, Not Just the Steps

Community reports consistently say the same thing about this track: the moves are simple, but the *performance* is what makes it look good. "No Scrubs" is a song with a smirk. If your face is blank and your shoulders are tense, the routine reads flat even with perfect timing.

Three quick fixes:

- **Chin up, shoulders back.** Posture alone can make a basic routine look polished.
- **Finish every pose.** Do not start drifting toward the next move before the current one lands.
- **Commit to the sass.** The finger points and head turns are the personality of the routine — hit them like you mean it.

## Scoring: Turning Good Timing into High Scores

Just Dance routines grade each beat on a tiered scale, moving from a missed input up through progressively better ratings until you reach the top grade. The exact thresholds vary by routine, but the strategy is universal: consistency beats occasional brilliance.

| Common Mistake | Why It Costs Points | The Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Holding the controller or phone loosely | Motion tracking reads sloppy movement as a miss | Keep a firm, natural grip and let your arm move freely |
| Rushing ahead of the beat | Early inputs register before the scoring window opens | Breathe and let the music lead you |
| Standing too close to the screen | Tracking sensors lose part of your silhouette | Step back so your full upper body stays visible |
| Over-dancing easy sections | Exaggerated motion breaks the tracking pose | Match the on-screen coach's range, not your maximum |
| Restarting after one bad section | You lose the rhythm of the full run | Play through — the chorus repeats and you can recover |

### Three Habits That Raise Every Score

- **Warm up first.** Two minutes of shoulder rolls and side steps before a run improves accuracy noticeably.
- **Use the practice feature.** Slowing down a section is faster than brute-forcing it at full speed.
- **Film one run.** Watching yourself once reveals timing habits you cannot feel in the moment.
