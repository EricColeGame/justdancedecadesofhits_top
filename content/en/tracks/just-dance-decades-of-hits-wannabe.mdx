## Tips for Scoring High on the Wannabe Routine

The routine rewards commitment over precision. Here is what actually moves your score:

- **Learn the chorus first.** It repeats, so every point you earn there multiplies across the song.
- **Commit to full arm extensions.** Half-hearted gestures are the most common reason for a "Good" rating instead of "Perfect."
- **Stay in your lane.** In a five-coach setup, drifting toward the center of the room puts you in another player's tracking zone.
- **Keep your whole body in frame.** Five players on one screen means less room per person, so step back further than you think you need to.
- **Watch the coach on your side of the screen.** Mirroring the wrong dancer is the fastest way to lose a combo.

Here are the mistakes that show up most often, along with the fix:

| Common Mistake | Why It Hurts | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rushing the verses | Throws off timing for the whole combo | Let the beat lead, not your memory |
| Freezing during transitions | Breaks the streak multiplier | Keep a small motion going between moves |
| Crowding the camera | Tracking drops players | Mark five spots before you start |
| Ignoring hand shapes | Loses precision points | Match the coach's hands, not just the feet |
| Quitting after one miss | Combo resets compound | Reset and rebuild immediately |

If you are playing solo, treat the quintet like a fitness routine: the pacing is faster than a standard single-coach track, so warm up with something slower before you attempt a high-score run.

## The Extreme Version: What Players Report

The pack includes an Extreme Version of "Wannabe," which follows the series convention of taking the same song and pushing the choreography into faster, denser territory. Community reports describe Extreme routines as endurance tests first and accuracy tests second — the moves come at you quickly enough that stamina becomes the limiting factor before precision does.

A few practical notes for anyone planning to attempt it:

- **Treat it as a separate track.** Your score on the standard quintet routine does not carry over.
- **Practice the transitions.** Extreme versions usually compress the downtime between moves, so the moments you would normally rest are where mistakes happen.
- **Play it after you have the standard version down.** The two routines share a song but not a rhythm map.

For most players, the standard version will be the one that gets played at gatherings, while the Extreme Version becomes a personal challenge to chip away at over multiple sessions.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Just Dance Decades of Hits Wannabe

**Is "Wannabe" by the Spice Girls in Just Dance: Decades of Hits?**

Yes. "Wannabe" is the 1990s spotlight track in the pack, and it comes with both a standard quintet routine and an Extreme Version. It is one of the headline songs used to promote the decade-by-decade concept.

The routine is a quintet, meaning five coaches appear on screen. Community documentation identifies it as the first quintet in the history of the Just Dance series, so it is designed for a full group rather than a solo dancer.

**Was "Wannabe" in Just Dance before?**
