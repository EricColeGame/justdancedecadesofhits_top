## Modes and Mini-Activities: What to Click First

The Just Dance Decades of Hits menu is not only about picking songs. Two menu layers sit alongside the tracklist, and both change how a session flows.

**Mini-activities** are short, low-stakes challenges that break up the dancing. They are ideal when your group is waiting on someone to arrive, or when a song ends and nobody can agree on the next one. Player experience suggests treating them as palate cleansers rather than the main event — two or three between longer stretches of dancing works well.

**Rewards and progression** are the long game. The store listing notes that exclusive rewards are tied to this edition specifically, which gives you a reason to revisit worlds you have already cleared instead of sticking to one favorite decade forever.

## Alternate Routines: The Hidden Layer of the Just Dance Decades of Hits Menu

Alternate routines are the most commonly missed feature in the entire game. They do not shout for attention, and many players finish a session without realizing a favorite song has a second, completely different choreography sitting behind it.

Community reports on the Just Dance wiki catalog several of these variants, including:

| Song | Alternate version | Type |
|---|---|---|
| "Hello" | Video Call Version | Duet |
| "HOT TO GO!" | Take Away Version | Duet |
| "NUEVAYoL" | Extreme Version | Solo |
| "Stronger" | Chair Dance Version | Solo |

The practical takeaway: when you highlight a song in the menu, check for a version selector before you confirm. If a routine has an alternate, the menu will usually let you swap between them on the same screen. Players who learn this early effectively double their library without buying anything extra.

There is also a difficulty angle. An Extreme Version of a track you already know is a genuinely different physical challenge — different pacing, more floor work, and far less margin for sloppy timing. If you are dancing with a mixed group, let the confident players take the extreme variant while everyone else stays on the standard routine. Nobody has to sit out.

## Menu Navigation Tips and a Quick-Start Workflow

A little menu discipline makes a big difference once four or five people are holding controllers. Here is a workflow that works well for a first session:

1. **Open the world map first, not the song list.** Choosing a decade gives the group a shared starting point and avoids the "what do you want to dance to" loop.
2. **Queue two songs per world, then rotate.** It keeps energy up and exposes everyone to different eras.
3. **Check for alternate routines on any song you love.** This is the single highest-value habit you can build.
4. **Use mini-activities as transitions.** They cover the awkward gap while someone grabs water or swaps players.
5. **Return to the rewards screen after every few songs.** Progress notifications are a surprisingly strong motivator for younger players.

| Player type | Where to start in the menu | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Total beginner | 2020s or 1970s world, standard solo | Familiar songs, readable choreography |
| Returning Just Dance veteran | Alternate routines and Extreme Versions | Fresh challenge from known tracks |
| Family with kids | Duets in the '70s and 2000s blocks | Shared moves, forgiving timing |
| Party host | World map plus mini-activities | Keeps a crowd moving between songs |
| Completionist | Rewards and progression screens | Tracks what is still locked |

One last tip: learn the back button. Jumping from a world into a song, then backing out to the world map, is much faster than scrolling the full tracklist when you change your mind mid-queue. Players who map that route early move through the Just Dance Decades of Hits menu noticeably faster than those who treat every screen as a dead end.

## FAQ

**How many worlds does the Just Dance Decades of Hits menu include?**
The official store description references six themed worlds, which matches the six decades the game covers — the 1970s through the 2020s. Each world groups its own songs, activities, and rewards.

**Do I need to dig through the menu to find alternate routines?**
Yes, and it is worth the effort. Alternate routines are usually attached to a song rather than listed separately, so you have to highlight a track and look for a version option. Community reports catalog variants such as a Video Call Version of "Hello" and an Extreme Version of "NUEVAYoL."

**Can I dance with a friend using the same menu?**
Absolutely. Duet routines are part of the standard lineup, and the game is explicitly marketed as something to play with friends and family. Look for routines labeled as duets, or pick any song and add a second player during setup.

**What should I click first if I have never played before?**
Start with the world map rather than the full song list. Pick a decade you recognize, choose a standard routine, and dance one song before exploring anything else. Once you have the rhythm of the Just Dance Decades of Hits menu down, alternate routines and mini-activities become easy to slot in.
