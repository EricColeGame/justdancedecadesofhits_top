### Why the Pack Format Helps Modes

Because the collection is organized by decade, playlist building becomes the single most valuable "mode" in practice. A 45-minute party set is easy to assemble: two 70s tracks, three 80s tracks, three 90s tracks, then a 2000s finale. That structure gives the room a narrative, and it gives slower dancers a reason to stay in for one more song.

### Which Mode to Try First

If you're playing alone, start in solo on a 1970s track — the slower phrasing lets you learn the scoring window without pressure. If you're hosting, open in co-op on a group-pop era song so nobody feels singled out. Save the online dance floor for after the room has warmed up, when a competitive score actually means something to everyone watching.

## Party Setup and Scoring Tips That Actually Help

The most common mistake hosts make with an era-based pack is treating it like a random shuffle. A little planning goes a long way.

| Step | What to Do | Why It Works |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Warm up with two 1970s tracks | Slower grooves teach the scoring window |
| 2 | Add one 1980s hip-hop track | Raises tempo without raising complexity too fast |
| 3 | Run a 1990s block in co-op | Group routines hide individual mistakes |
| 4 | Finish with a 2000s or later track | High-energy closer keeps people standing |
| 5 | Save the online dance floor for last | Competitive stakes land better once everyone's loose |

A few additional habits help more than any single setting:

- **Give each player one "pick."** Letting people choose their own decade keeps reluctant dancers invested.
- **Use playlists instead of manual selection.** Fewer menus means more dancing.
- **Watch the coach's silhouette, not the lyrics.** Movement cues appear before the lyric line resolves.
- **Reset your calibration if scores feel random.** Sync issues are the most common complaint in player experience threads.
- **Keep sessions to about 45 minutes.** Six decades is a lot of movement, and fatigue hurts accuracy more than unfamiliarity does.

For scoring specifically, consistency beats flair. Just Dance rewards holding a pose through its full window rather than snapping into it and moving on early — a habit that matters even more on 1980s and 2010s tracks where the beats come fast.

Co-op or group modes, paired with 1970s and 1980s songs. Shared scoring means a less experienced dancer can't single-handedly tank the round, and the older tracks are more forgiving on timing.
