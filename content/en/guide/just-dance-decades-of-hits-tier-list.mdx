## Why a Just Dance Decades of Hits Tier List Is Worth Building

Anyone who has spent an evening passing controllers around the room knows the argument: which routines are genuinely great, and which ones get skipped the second the song wheel lands on them? A **Just Dance Decades of Hits tier list** turns that endless debate into a single image you can share, defend, and revisit. Instead of trading vague opinions, your group gets a ranked snapshot of the whole lineup.

That matters because the game's entire hook is its range. It pulls from several eras of pop at once, so one player's nostalgia trip is another player's unfamiliar track. A **Just Dance Decades of Hits tier list** gives everyone a shared vocabulary — top tier, mid tier, skip tier — which makes planning a party setlist or a solo cardio session much faster.

### Tier labels you will see in the wild

Community templates vary. Some use the classic S-through-F scale, while others use playful custom row labels. According to community reports, one widely used TierMaker template for this game covers the main songlist plus new Just Dance+ additions and uses rows labeled YEAH, Perfect, Super, Good, Ok, and X.

| Community row label | Rough S–F equivalent | What it signals |
| --- | --- | --- |
| YEAH | S | Instantly replayable, always in the setlist |
| Perfect | A | Great choreography, minor nitpicks only |
| Super | B | Solid fun, rarely the reason you boot the game |
| Good | C | Fine once, forgettable after |
| Ok | D | Filler energy, easy to skip |
| X | F | Actively awkward or boring to play |

The exact labels do not matter as much as consistency. Pick a scale, apply it to every song, and resist the urge to reshuffle mid-list just because your favorite landed in B tier.

## The Criteria That Decide Every Placement

Most tier lists fall apart because the creator never defines what "good" means. Before you drag a single icon, decide what you are actually measuring. The framework below works well for this game because it blends dance quality with the practical reality of playing at home.

| Criterion | Suggested weight | What to look for | Score range |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Choreography quality | 25% | Readable moves, good flow, satisfying combos | 1–10 |
| Music and beat | 20% | Strong hook, clear rhythm, doesn't drag | 1–10 |
| Coach and visual presentation | 20% | Memorable coach, clean staging, readable cues | 1–10 |
| Replayability and fitness value | 20% | Sweat factor, energy curve, would you pick it twice? | 1–10 |
| Era appeal and nostalgia | 15% | Does the decade identity come through? | 1–10 |

A quick way to use this: score each song out of 50, then sort. Anything 42+ is your YEAH tier. Anything below 25 lands in X. The weights are a starting point, not gospel — if you play mostly for exercise, bump replayability and fitness value up to 30% and drop nostalgia to 5%.

### Why choreography outranks the song itself

A great track with clumsy choreography is a bad routine. A mediocre track with a brilliant routine can carry a whole session. Watch for three things: whether the moves sync to the beat you actually hear, whether the coach's body blocks the on-screen prompts, and whether the routine has a satisfying "money move" you remember afterward. Those three details separate an A-tier pick from a C-tier one far more reliably than genre preference does.

## Decade-by-Decade: How Each Era Changes the Verdict

Because the lineup spans multiple eras, judging every song by the same yardstick is a mistake. Each decade brings different strengths to a dance routine.

| Era | Signature traits | Tier-list strengths | Tier-list weaknesses |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1980s | Synth hooks, bold poses, big arm work | Iconic moves, high nostalgia | Repetitive loops can feel stiff |
| 1990s | Pop anthems, group choreography | Strong singalong energy | Slower tempos for solo play |
| 2000s | Club beats, fast footwork | High sweat factor | Busy steps, harder to read |
| 2010s | EDM drops, viral hooks | Big payoff moments | Drop-heavy sections can stall |
| 2020s | Modern pop, short-form dance trends | Familiar, snappy choreography | Less nostalgic pull for older players |

### Score each era against its own goals

An 80s routine should be judged on whether it captures that era's theatrical confidence. A 2000s club track should be judged on whether it gets your heart rate up. Applying 2020s standards to a 1980s song usually produces a tier list that feels wrong to everyone reading it.

### Where JD+ songs fit into the ranking

Just Dance+ adds a rotating stream of extra tracks on top of the base songlist, and community templates increasingly include those additions as separate entries. Treat them as a distinct category at first.

| Song source | Availability | Effect on your tier list |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main songlist | Always available with the game | Safe anchor for your ranking |
| Just Dance+ additions | Requires an active subscription | Separate them so rankings stay fair |
| Returning classics | Rotates in and out | Re-check placements periodically |

The practical move is to build one master list and one JD+ list. Otherwise a fantastic subscription-only routine can push a main-list song down a tier for no good reason, and anyone reading your list without a subscription will be confused.

## Step-by-Step: Build Your Own Just Dance Decades of Hits Tier List

Here is a workflow that takes about an hour and produces a list you will actually stand behind.

| Step | Action | Time | Tip |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Pick a template with all songs and JD+ entries | 5 min | Confirm the template matches your game version |
| 2 | Write your tier criteria and weights | 5 min | Do this before ranking anything |
| 3 | Score every song out of 50 | 25 min | Score blind — hide the song title if you can |
| 4 | Sort into tiers by score bands | 5 min | Use fixed cutoffs, not gut feel |
| 5 | Play your top and bottom tiers once more | 15 min | Reality-check the extremes |
| 6 | Publish, then revisit after a month | 5 min | Fresh ears catch bad placements |

Step three is the one people skip, and it is the one that matters most. Scoring songs one at a time, in a fixed order, prevents the "recency bias" problem where the last routine you played feels better than it is.

### Reality-checking your extremes

Play your top three and bottom three songs back to back. If a bottom-tier pick suddenly feels fun, you probably scored the choreography too harshly. If a top-tier pick bores you on the second run, replayability was overrated in your scoring. Adjust and re-sort — the goal is a list that survives a second playthrough, not one that looks clever.

## Common Tier List Mistakes and How to Fix Them

Even experienced players make the same handful of errors. Here is how to avoid them.

| Mistake | Why it hurts | The fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ranking by song you like, not routine you enjoy | Produces a music chart, not a dance tier list | Score choreography first |
| Mixing JD+ songs with the main list | Skews availability for readers | Keep two separate lists |
| Too many tiers | Nothing feels distinct | Cap it at five or six rows |
| No criteria written down | Impossible to defend placements | Publish your weights alongside the list |
| Ranking all at once from memory | Recency bias dominates | Score song by song, in order |
| Never updating | New additions go unranked | Revisit after each content drop |

### How to read other people's tier lists

Community rankings are useful data, not verdicts. When you open someone else's list, check three things: whether they separated Just Dance+ content, whether they explained their criteria, and whether their top tier matches the songs you personally replay. If all three line up, their list is a good reference. If none do, you are looking at a personal mood board — enjoyable, but not a guide.

## FAQ

**Is there an official Just Dance Decades of Hits tier list?**

No. Tier lists are entirely community-made, and every one reflects the creator's own taste and criteria. The template most players start from is a fan creation that covers the main songlist and new Just Dance+ tracks, but the placements inside it are subjective. Treat any tier list you find as a strong opinion, not a scoreboard.

**How many tiers should my Just Dance Decades of Hits tier list have?**

Five or six rows is the sweet spot. Fewer than four and you cannot separate the great from the merely good; more than seven and the distinctions stop meaning anything. If you want a quick scale, use YEAH, Perfect, Super, Good, Ok, and X — the same row labels seen in popular community templates.

**Should I include Just Dance+ songs in the same ranking?**

Build two lists. Subscription-only routines are not available to everyone, so folding them into the main ranking makes your placements harder to interpret. Rank the base songlist first, then rank JD+ additions separately and note which ones would crack your top tier if availability were not a factor.

**How often should I update my tier list?**

After every content update and roughly once a month otherwise. Choreography that felt awkward on your first attempt often clicks on the third, and nostalgia fades fast. A short revisit keeps your Just Dance Decades of Hits tier list accurate instead of a snapshot of one weekend.

**Do tier lists actually help you get better at the game?**

Indirectly, yes. Ranking songs forces you to think about move readability, tempo, and energy management, which makes you more aware of your own technique. Building a "practice tier" of routines that train footwork or stamina is one of the most underrated uses of a tier list.
