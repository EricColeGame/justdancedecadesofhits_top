## Setup Essentials: Controllers, Space, and Difficulty Settings

Most first-session frustration is a setup problem, not a skill problem. Before you judge your dancing, spend five minutes on the room and the hardware.

Give yourself a full arm's length of clearance on every side, and make sure the floor is not slick. If you are on a hard surface, socks are a hazard; bare feet or grippy shoes are safer. Point any camera toward an evenly lit area, and keep the music loud enough that you can hear the beat over your own footsteps.

| Setup option | What you need | Strengths | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Motion controller (Joy-Con or similar) | Console plus one controller per player | Precise hand tracking, works in small rooms | Battery life, and always use the wrist strap |
| Smartphone app | Phone on the same network | No extra hardware, great for groups | Lighting, phone placement, and notifications interrupting play |
| Camera-based tracking | Console camera | Controller-free and hands-free | Needs space, good lighting, and no furniture in frame |

Two settings deserve a mention. First, run the audio-lag or calibration option if your version offers one — a small delay between the speaker and the screen will quietly wreck your timing. Second, check whether your version has a difficulty, coaching, or no-fail style option. Turning assist features on is not cheating; it is how you learn a routine's shape before you try to score on it.

## How Scoring Works and How to Earn More Stars

Just Dance scores you on timing and motion, not on how good you look. Each move is judged as you hit it, points accumulate through the song, and your total converts into a star rating at the end, with higher tiers available for near-perfect runs.

Here is the part that surprises most newcomers: in most setups, the game is reading the controller in your hand more than your feet. That is why arm paths and hand positions matter more than fancy footwork. If you nail every arm movement and shuffle through the legs, you will usually outscore someone doing the opposite.

| Scoring factor | What it rewards | Beginner fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Timing | Hitting the move on the beat | Turn the music up and count along |
| Hand position | Where the controller sits at the moment of the move | Mirror the coach's arms, not their feet |
| Continuity | Keeping movement through the entire routine | Never freeze after a miss |
| Bonus or highlighted moves | Extra points on marked moves | Save a little energy for the chorus |
| Star thresholds | Total points per song | Replay the same song to learn its patterns |

A practical target for a first week: aim for three stars on three different songs rather than five stars on one. Three stars across a range of tempos builds far more transferable rhythm than grinding a single routine.

## Picking Beginner-Friendly Songs in Just Dance Decades of Hits

Most Just Dance entries display a difficulty rating next to each song, and that rating is your best friend early on. Start at the bottom of the scale and work upward. A song rated easy is not a lesser experience — it is a tutorial with a great soundtrack.

Beyond the rating, pick songs you already know. Familiarity is a hidden difficulty modifier: if you can hum the chorus, you already know when the beat drops and where the routine will speed up.

| Song type | Why it helps beginners | What to look for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Slow, big-arm pop tracks | Fewer direction changes, readable shapes | A relaxed tempo and wide movements |
| Songs you already know | You anticipate the beat instinctively | A chorus you can hum |
| Symmetrical duets | Two players learn the same shapes at once | Mirrored left/right choreography |
| Low-rated routines | Designed for first-timers | A low difficulty rating on the song card |
| Tracks with a clear intro | Time to find the rhythm before scoring | An obvious count-in or instrumental opening |

Community reports from long-time players consistently suggest the same habit: play one song three times in a row before moving on. The first run teaches you the shape, the second fixes your timing, and the third is where your score jumps. Player experience also suggests that a 20-minute session beats a 90-minute marathon when you are still learning, because accuracy drops sharply once your arms get tired.

## Your First-Week Practice Plan

You do not need a strict regimen, but a loose plan prevents the classic beginner pattern of playing randomly and never improving. Try this structure and adjust the days to fit your schedule.

| Day | Focus | Session length | Goal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Setup, calibration, and three easy songs | 20 minutes | Find a comfortable stance |
| 2 | Replay the same three songs | 20 minutes | Beat your own scores |

| 4 | Stretch or light play only | 10 minutes | Let your shoulders recover |

| 6 | Duet or group session | 30 minutes | Have fun and stop score-chasing |

### Common Beginner Mistakes and How to Fix Them

| Mistake | Why it costs points | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Watching the coach's feet | Scoring usually reads the controller in your hand | Track the arm path instead |
| Restarting after every miss | Breaks rhythm and wastes the song | Keep moving and finish the routine |
| Playing a brand-new song every round | You never learn a routine's patterns | Repeat each song two to three times |
| Holding the controller still | Motion tracking needs movement | Keep the controller flowing with the beat |
| Skipping calibration | Audio delay throws off your timing | Calibrate once per new setup |

The through-line in all of these is patience. Just Dance Decades of Hits rewards repetition far more than raw athleticism, and the players who look effortless on video are usually the ones who played the same routine twenty times.

## FAQ

**Do I need dance experience to enjoy Just Dance Decades of Hits?**

No. The routines are choreographed to be readable from the screen, and the difficulty ratings let you start at the easiest tier. Most players feel noticeably more coordinated after about a week of short sessions, even with zero dance background.

**How many stars should a beginner expect?**

Three stars on an easy or medium-rated song is a solid first-week result. Five-star and higher-tier runs usually come after you have memorized a routine's structure, so treat them as a milestone rather than a starting expectation.

**Can I play Just Dance Decades of Hits alone?**

Yes. Solo play is actually the fastest way to improve, because you can repeat songs without holding up a group. Many players split their time: solo sessions for practice, group sessions for fun.

**What is the single most useful tip in a Just Dance Decades of Hits beginner guide?**

Focus on your arms and your timing before your footwork. The controller in your hand drives most of your score, so clean arm paths and steady rhythm will raise your stars faster than trying to copy every step perfectly.
